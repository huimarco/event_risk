    What this is

    A live map from geopolitical questions people bet money on to the investments those questions affect — so a risk desk can see which world events its portfolio is exposed to, which way each would push it, and how the odds are moving.

    Prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket put a price on questions like "Will China invade Taiwan by the end of 2027?". A price of 9¢ for a contract that pays $1 if the answer is yes means traders collectively put the odds at about 9%. This project collects those odds for every geopolitical market, works out which markets are really the same question, connects each question to the countries, people and organisations it involves, and from there to macro factors (credit spreads, currencies, oil, inflation, GDP), the niche factors that drive each industry's revenue and costs (airline load factor, jet fuel, bank margins, wafer demand), GICS industries, and the issuers — companies and other borrowers — exposed to them.

    Why it matters

    • Geopolitical risk is usually described, not measured. Research notes say a conflict is "a risk"; they rarely say how likely it is today, whether that changed this week, or which holdings it touches. Prediction markets supply the missing number — a probability, updated continuously, with money behind it.
    • The odds are scattered and hard to use. The same question is listed on several venues in different wording; thousands of contracts expire, get renamed or vanish. Nobody links them to portfolios. This project does the cleaning, matching and linking.
    • The history can't be rebuilt later. Venues keep only part of their past prices and none of their past contract terms. Everything here is stored as it was known at each moment, so past days can be replayed later — essential for testing whether the signal would have helped.

    It is built as risk-measurement infrastructure, not a trading signal: the consensus probability is the point — a shared, auditable number a risk committee can watch — and raw history is available to quants who want to build their own models.

    A worked example: selecting the issuer TSMC shows "Will China invade Taiwan?" near the top — ▼ impact −1.46, odds 9.5% by the end of 2027. The path reads: the question involves Taiwan as the target of an invasion (adverse for Taiwan) → TSMC is exposed to Taiwan (its fabs are there) → so a YES is adverse for TSMC; the measured sensitivity of each TSMC listing (TSM, 2330.TW) shows how much its shares actually moved on past jumps in those odds. A ceasefire question would show the opposite sign for a defense contractor like Lockheed Martin.

    Two routes lead from a question to an issuer: through the entities it is exposed to (Taiwan → TSMC), and through factors: question → macro factor → niche factor → the issuer's GICS industry (Hormuz closure → Brent → jet fuel cost → Passenger Airlines → Delta).

    Coverage: about 10,900 geopolitical markets, grouped into about 4,800 questions (around 2,000 trading at any time), linked to 1,450 entities and 517 issuers (S&P 500 companies plus Asian chipmakers and tech; add your own borrowers in issuers.csv). Everything is produced by algorithms from public data — no AI models — except the analyst estimates marked below.

    Step 1 · Collect the markets

    Kalshi's world and politics markets and Polymarket's geopolitics section are read several times a day: prices (bid, ask, last trade), open interest, and the full contract terms, with hourly price history back to 2021. A market is in scope if Polymarket files it under geopolitics, or — for Kalshi — if it involves a foreign state actor: a country other than the US, or a person or organisation belonging to one. "Will Trump visit China?" is in; "Will the Senate confirm…?" is out.

    Step 2 · Markets → propositions: is it the same bet?

    Questions that read alike are often different bets ("visits China before May 14" vs "on May 14"), so wording similarity is not used. Each market is parsed into a contract signature:

    FieldExample: "Will Trump visit Taiwan before Jan 1, 2027?"
    Actionvisit — one of ~50 event types (invade, strike, blockade, sanction, ceasefire, meet, leave office, appoint…)
    Actor → targetDonald Trump → Taiwan (which side of the verb matters)
    Time windowDec 2, 2025 – Dec 31, 2026, read from the resolution rules
    Threshold— (used for "tariff ≥ 25%", "more than 260 ships", "20–21 seats")
    Conditionsin person, phone calls count, becomes law, negated ("…agrees not to…") …

    Two markets become one proposition only if every field matches, and then real money gets the last word: if their prices differ by more than 10 points they are linked as near-equivalent instead, never averaged. Other safeguards: a negated question never matches the plain one; on the same venue the resolution rules are compared too, so two identical questions about different places are kept apart; each pair is judged once, at the moment the newer market was listed, so decisions never flip later.

    OutcomeMeaning
    mergedThe same bet as another market; one proposition, prices averaged (weighted by open interest).
    linkedA similar but different bet (other deadline, threshold or wording): connected, prices kept separate.
    unmatchedRead fine, but no other market asks exactly this — the usual case, since venues rarely list identical questions.
    unparsedThe type of event isn't recognised, so it can't be compared with other markets (about one market in eight). It still appears as its own question.

    The Events tab lists every market (contract) by outcome, with its parsed signature and the reason each near miss was rejected. Decisions can be reviewed and overridden by hand. Two markets from the same venue with different deadlines are never merged, even when each matches a third market from the other venue.

    Step 2b · Propositions → questions: one row per real-world question

    Venues list the same question many times with different deadlines ("Will China invade Taiwan by June 2026 / December 2026 / December 2027?") or, for counts and levels, different thresholds ("more than 15 / 20 / 25 ships through Hormuz in the week of Oct 19–25"). Each of these is a separate bet — a proposition — but a risk desk thinks of them as one question. Propositions whose contract signatures are identical except for the deadline become rungs of one question; for threshold series, each measurement period (each week) is its own question with the thresholds as rungs, because consecutive weeks are separate events. Different outcomes listed side by side ("Person O / Person P as head of state", same deadline) stay separate questions.

    • The rungs form a curve: 2.1% by end-2026, 5.5% by mid-2027, 9.5% by end-2027 for a Chinese invasion of Taiwan.
    • The headline probability is the most-traded live rung (24-hour volume, then open interest) — the one with the most money behind it.
    • Live or closed: a rung is live if it traded in the last 7 days; a question is live while any rung is. Closed rungs stay visible as history.
    • Entities, issuers and factors attach to questions, once, instead of to each rung.

    Step 3 · Questions → entities: who and what is involved

    There is no hand-made list of countries or leaders; entities are rediscovered from the questions on every update.

    • Finding them. Every name-like phrase in a question ("Xi Jinping", "Strait of Hormuz", "ballistic missile") is looked up as a Wikipedia article title; the longest phrase that matches wins. A lone surname ("Xi") resolves to the one person already found with that name; a nationality word ("Chinese") to its country.
    • Knowing what they are. Wikidata, Wikipedia's structured sister project, says whether each is a person, country, organisation or concept, and which country it belongs to (Trump → United States), so "Trump makes a deal" and "the U.S. makes a deal" are recognised as the same side.
    • Their role in the question. From the contract signature: actor (China, in "China invades Taiwan"), target (Taiwan), party (both sides of a meeting), or concept (invasion, tariffs).
    • Connecting entities to each other. Wikidata facts (Xi Jinping — citizen of — China) plus a strength score from how often Wikipedia articles mention both:
    relatedness(A, B) = 1 − [ log max(|A|,|B|) − log |A∩B| ] / [ log W − log min(|A|,|B|) ]
    |A|, |B| = articles mentioning each · |A∩B| = articles mentioning both · W = all articles (~7.3M) — the Normalized Google Distance, computed on Wikipedia

    Example: Putin–Xi Jinping 0.66, China–Taiwan 0.56. Links need at least 10 articles mentioning both and a score of 0.25 or more. Concepts are kept only if specific (mentioned in under 1% of articles) and related to the question's actors — "tariff" yes, "company" no.

    Step 4 · Entities → issuers

    The graph stops at the issuer: the company or borrower, not its individual bonds, loans or shares — you already map issuers to the instruments you hold. Each issuer's listed securities are linked to it as reference data, and how much each security actually moves with a question is measured from prices, not assumed (see Securities in the API docs). Which issuers depend on which entities — Taiwan → TSMC, Strait of Hormuz → oil producers — is the one hand-written input: analyst estimates (0–1) based on each company's industry and headquarters. A second small table says which industries gain when things get worse on a channel: defense contractors from conflict, oil producers from supply disruption. Everything else suffers with the entity it depends on.

    Step 4b · Questions → macro factors → niche factors → GICS industries

    The second route works through what moves companies' earnings rather than who they depend on. It is written by hand for now — analyst priors, not measured:

    • Macro factors (about 90): market prices measured every day — equity indices, currencies, Brent, gas, metals, grains, freight, Treasury yields, credit spreads, the VIX, inflation breakevens — and economic indicators released monthly or quarterly (CPI, core PCE, GDP, payrolls, PMIs, housing starts, trade volumes), which are linked but not measured. A question links to one by rule: "the US raises tariffs" → US CPI, import prices, GDP; "Iran is struck" → Brent.
    • Niche factors (about 370): each industry's revenue, cost and investment drivers — for airlines available seat miles, load factor, yield, jet fuel, cost per seat mile; for banks loan growth, net interest margin, deposit beta, credit losses; for chipmakers shipments, ASPs, wafer demand, foundry orders, fab utilization, export controls. Each says whether a higher value helps or hurts earnings.
    • Transmission (about 360 signed links): macro factors drive niche factors (Brent ↑ → jet fuel ↑; mortgage rates ↑ → housing affordability ↓), and niche factors drive GICS industries (jet fuel ↑ hurts Passenger Airlines). Issuers sit in their GICS sub-industry, so an issuer's detail lists what drives its earnings.

    Where a question's move in a macro factor has been measured (factor sensitivities, see Factors in the API docs), the industry view also shows which way earnings would go on a YES.

    Step 5 · Direction and size of impact

    Each question's link to an entity records what a YES means for it. The event type sets a channel (conflict, trade, sanctions, instability, diplomacy), a severity (an invasion 1.0, a missile test 0.4, a meeting 0.2) and a direction by role: an invasion is adverse for the target and somewhat adverse for the invader; a ceasefire or trade deal is favourable for both; a negated or reversed question ("…ends", "…lifted") flips it. An issuer's exposure to a question adds up every path through the graph:

    path strength = severity × role weight × pass-through × exposure weight
    path sign = direction for the entity × how the issuer is affected on that channel
    impact = Σ sign × strength over all paths · relevance = 1 − Π(1 − strength) · risk = |impact| × √(p (1 − p))
    pass-through = 1 for the entity itself, 0.5 × relatedness for a linked entity (one hop)

    Risk is highest for questions near 50% — a 99% event has little left to move.

    How to read the impact numbers

    Impact is an estimate from the graph's logic — which entities a question involves, and how each issuer depends on them — in relative units, not percentage returns. It is not measured from price history, and tests against past price moves have not yet confirmed it. Use it to see which questions matter for an issuer and in which direction, not as a forecast of how much its securities will move.

    Numbers you will see

    ProbabilityFor a rung (proposition): the latest price of its markets, averaged (weighted by open interest when available); only same-bet markets are averaged. For a question: its headline rung — the most-traded live one.
    24h changeProbability now minus 24 hours earlier, in points.
    RelevanceHow strongly an issuer is connected to a question, 0–1, combining every path.
    ImpactSigned: ▲ the issuer is expected to benefit if the event happens, ▼ to suffer; "direction unknown" when the event type has no direction. A prior, not a measured beta.
    Risk|impact| × √(p(1−p)): how much the question can still move the issuer at today's odds.

    Data and updates

    MarketsKalshi and Polymarket — prices updated several times a day, with hourly history back to 2021
    ConnectionsUpdated when new markets are listed or their terms change; price moves never alter them. Every change is kept, so past views stay exact
    EntitiesWikipedia and Wikidata, refreshed monthly
    Factor pricesDaily closes: equity indices, FX and commodities (Yahoo), credit spreads and yields (FRED)

    Limitations

    • Which issuers depend on which entities, and how they are affected, are analyst estimates rather than sourced figures.
    • Direction of impact comes from the type of event; questions whose event type isn't recognised show relevance but no direction.
    • Issuer coverage is deepest for US–China, Taiwan and semiconductors; questions about other regions may have few linked issuers — use factors for those.
    • Prices on prediction markets can be thin: a small trade can move a quiet market, so check volume before reading much into a jump.

    Use it in Excel

    The Excel add-in brings these probabilities and exposures into your own spreadsheets, and keeps them up to date. Setting it up takes about five minutes. You don't need admin rights or any technical knowledge.

    Before you start: you need the Microsoft 365 version of Excel. To check, open Excel and click File → Account: under "Product Information" it should say Microsoft 365. The steps below are for Windows; for a Mac or Excel in a web browser, see further down.

    Install it (Windows)

    1. Close Excel if it is open (save your work first).
    2. Download the installer: Download the Event Risk installer.
      Your browser may warn that the file "isn't commonly downloaded". This is normal for small installers. In Microsoft Edge: point at the download, click the ⋯ (three dots) → Keep → Show more → Keep anyway. In Chrome: click Keep.
    3. Open the file you downloaded — click it in your browser's list of downloads, or find install-event-risk.cmd in your Downloads folder and double-click it.
      If a blue box says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. If a box asks "Do you want to run this file?", click Run.
    4. A black window opens and says "Done! The add-in is installed." Press any key (for example the space bar) to close it.
    5. Open Excel with a blank workbook. On the Home tab, at the far right, click the Event Risk button. A panel opens on the right of the window.
      Can't see the button? Make the Excel window wider, or click Home → Add-ins and choose Event Risk under Developer Add-ins. If Excel asks whether to allow it, click yes.
    6. Check the panel says "Connected." under the Refresh button. If it mentions an API key, paste the key you were given into the API key box further down, click Save, then click Refresh all Event Risk formulas.

    Try it

    1. Build a watch list. In the panel, type taiwan in the search box and press Enter. Click an empty cell in your sheet, then click a question in the panel: Excel fills in a row with the question's id, its wording, today's probability, its change over the last 24 hours and which deadline the probability is for. Click more questions to add more rows underneath.
    2. Use a formula. Click an empty cell, type =ER.QUESTIONS("taiwan", 10) and press Enter. A table of questions fills in below and to the right — leave those cells empty so it has room.
    3. Point at the id. Every table's first column is an id (it starts with q:, prop: or mkt:). You never type ids: point a formula at the cell, e.g. =ER.PROB(A2). In the Explore tab, Copy id on any question, rung or contract copies its id to paste into Excel.

    Formulas

    All the formulas start with =ER. — as you type it, Excel lists them and explains each part.

    FormulaWhat you get
    =ER.QUESTIONS("iran")A table of questions mentioning Iran, biggest moves today first. The first column is each question's id, used by the formulas below.
    =ER.ENTITY("Iran")Every question involving a country, person or organisation — including ones that don't name it in the title — busiest first.
    =ER.PROB(A2)The probability (as a decimal: 0.25 = 25%) for the id in cell A2. =ER.CHANGE(A2) gives its 24-hour change and =ER.NAME(A2) its wording.
    =ER.FIELD(A2, "updated")One detail: updated (time of the latest price — format the cell as a date), volume_24h, open_interest, status, headline, close_time; for a single contract (an mkt: id) also title, rules, rules_secondary, resolution_sources.
    =ER.RUNGS(A2)Every deadline of that question with its own probability — for example by June, by December, by next year — and each one's id.
    =ER.CONTRACTS(A2)The actual Kalshi and Polymarket contracts behind it: venue, wording, price, trading volume and the full rules.
    =ER.HISTORY(A2)The probability day by day over the last 30 days. Format the first column as a date.
    =ER.CURVE(A2)For a question with deadlines: the chance in each period, the chance in that period if it hasn't happened yet, and the yearly rate — like a default curve.
    =ER.PROB_BY(A2, B2)The chance it happens by the date in B2 — for example before a bond in your portfolio matures.
    =ER.FACTORS(A2)The markets the question moves (oil, credit spreads, currencies, …) and by how much: per +1.0 of probability, and if it resolved YES or NO today. Units are % or bp.
    =ER.FACTOR("credit_us_hy")A factor's latest level (here the US high-yield spread, in %). Add "units", "name" or "series" for details.
    =ER.SENSITIVITY(A2, "brent")How much one factor moves per +1.0 of the question's probability.
    =ER.STRESS(A2, D2:D10, E2:E10)Your P&L if the question resolved YES now: factor ids in D, your P&L per 1% (or per 1 bp) of each factor in E. Add "no" for the NO case. Your numbers stay in Excel. The panel's Stress sheet button builds this layout for a question.

    Which probability does a question give? A question like "Will China invade Taiwan?" has several deadlines (by 2026, by 2027, …). Its probability is the headline deadline: the one with the most trading in the last 24 hours. The headline can switch as trading moves, so to follow one deadline over time use its id from ER.RUNGS. =ER.FIELD(A2, "headline") shows which deadline you're getting.

    The numbers update whenever Excel recalculates, or when you click Refresh all Event Risk formulas in the panel.

    Using the API

    Everything in the explorer is available as JSON over HTTP. Base URL: BASE. Interactive reference with a "try it" button for every endpoint: /docs.

    Basics

    AuthenticationIf the server has API keys configured, send yours in the X-API-Key header. A missing or wrong key returns 401.
    IDsQuestions q:polymarket:253889 · propositions (rungs) prop:polymarket:665270 · entities wd:Q865 (Wikidata id; Taiwan) · issuers iss:Q713418 (TSMC) · markets mkt:kalshi:KXTAIWANLVL4-29JAN01. Get them from the list endpoints.
    Probabilities0–1. prob is the blended price of a proposition's markets; change_24h is in probability units (0.05 = 5 points).
    Live onlyEvery question and proposition has status = live (priced in the last 7 days) or closed. List endpoints return live items unless include_closed=true.
    Errors401 bad key · 404 id not found · 422 invalid parameter.

    Quick start

    curl -H "X-API-Key: $KEY" "BASE/propositions?q=taiwan&sort=move&limit=5"
    import requests
api = requests.Session(); api.headers["X-API-Key"] = KEY      # omit if the server has no keys
top = api.get("BASE/propositions", params={"sort": "move", "limit": 10}).json()
for p in top["propositions"]:
    print(f'{p["prob"]:.1%}  {p["change_24h"] or 0:+.1%}  {p["name"]}')

    Questions

    GET/questions

    List questions with their headline probability (most-traded live rung) and its 24h change, 24h volume and open interest (summed over live rungs) and updated (the headline's latest price time). Params: q, entity (id or name, e.g. Iran), sort = move · prob · name, limit, include_closed.

    { "count": 1968, "questions": [
    { "id": "q:polymarket:253889", "name": "Will China invade Taiwan?", "kind": "deadlines", "n_rungs": 8, "n_live": 3,
      "status": "live", "prob": 0.095, "change_24h": 0.0, "headline": { "id": "prop:polymarket:…", "name": "…by December 31, 2027?" } } ] }
    GET/questions/{id}

    One question: every rung (window, threshold, probability, 24h change, status, 24h volume), the entities it involves, with role and direction, its factors with their measured sensitivity (see Factors), and coherence: ok plus any violations, rung pairs whose prices break the ladder's logic by more than 2 points (P(by later) ≥ P(by earlier); P(> higher) ≤ P(> lower)).

    GET/questions/{id}/curve

    A deadline question as a term structure, for setting event risk against a bond's maturity or a CDS curve. Per live deadline after today: prob (blended price), fitted (made monotone by isotonic regression), period_prob (chance in that period), conditional_prob (the same, given it hasn't happened before: a per-period hazard) and hazard_annual. Params: date → prob_by = P(event by that date), hazard constant within each period; extrapolate=true extends the last hazard past the last deadline.

    { "points": [ { "deadline": "2026-12-31", "prob": 0.031, "fitted": 0.031, "period_prob": 0.031, "conditional_prob": 0.031, "hazard_annual": 0.18 },
              { "deadline": "2027-12-31", "prob": 0.095, "fitted": 0.095, "period_prob": 0.064, "conditional_prob": 0.066, "hazard_annual": 0.068 } ],
  "prob_by": { "date": "2027-06-30", "prob": 0.063, "extrapolated": false } }
    GET/review/ladders

    Live multi-rung questions whose rung prices contradict the ladder, largest gap first: each is a wrong grouping or a mispricing.

    GET/questions/{id}/contracts

    Every venue contract behind the question's live rungs (latest deadline first): id, rung, venue, name, relation (same_as = blended into the rung's probability, near_equivalent = similar terms, kept separate), prob, volume_24h, open_interest, updated, close_time, rules. Param: include_closed.

    Propositions (rungs)

    GET/propositions

    List propositions with probability and 24h change. Params: q (name search), entity (only those involving this entity: id or name), sort = move (largest |24h change|, default) · prob · name, limit (≤ 500), include_closed.

    { "count": 151, "propositions": [
    { "id": "prop:polymarket:567621", "name": "Will China invade Taiwan by end of 2026?",
      "prob": 0.0215, "prob_24h_ago": 0.0205, "change_24h": 0.001,
      "close_time": "2027-01-01 04:59:00+00:00", "venues": ["polymarket"] } ] }
    GET/propositions/{id}

    One proposition: its venue markets (price, rules text), its question, the question's entities, and logical links (implies, mutex, near_equivalent) to other propositions, with the reason for each.

    GET/propositions/{id}/history

    Blended probability over time. Params: start, end (default: last 30 days), interval = 1h · 1d. Each point: {"t", "prob", "n_markets"}.

    Graph

    GET/graph/{node_id}

    The neighbourhood of any question, proposition, market, entity, issuer, factor (macro or niche; node layer) or GICS industry (gics:), for visualisation: nodes (id, type, name, depth, prob) and edges (src, dst, type, weight, provenance, note). Params: depth (1–3), fanout (strongest links kept per node).

    GET/propositions/{id}/graph

    Same neighbourhood format as /graph/{node_id} but rooted on a proposition (rung). Useful when you have a prop: id and want to explore its connections directly.

    Factors

    GET/factors

    The factor catalog in two layers: macro (about 90: country_equity · fx · commodity · rates · credit · industry · inflation · growth · labour · consumer · housing · trade · fiscal · volatility) and niche (about 370 industry drivers; family = revenue · cost · investment, plus earnings_sign and the gics codes they apply to), with how many questions are relevant to each. Params: layer, family. Each row: id, name, layer, family, definition, series (where the level comes from: yahoo:BZ=F, fred:BAMLH0A0HYM2), units (% = percent change, bp = basis points), level, level_date, n_questions, n_live_questions, n_measured (links with measured events), n_drives, n_driven_by, n_industries. Economic indicators (CPI, GDP…) have no series: they are released monthly or quarterly, too slowly for the daily measurement.

    GET/factors/{id}

    One factor — e.g. credit_us_hy, brent, equity_taiwan — with its latest level, every question relevant to it (live first, then by 24h volume), the paths that link each (question → entity [→ its country] → factor, with channel and role) and its sensitivity: beta = the factor's move per +1.0 of the question's probability in the factor's units (per_10pts = per 10 points), se, move_if_yes / move_if_no = the move if it resolved now, n_events and the events it was measured on. pooled gives, per channel, the typical move per +1.0 of adverse probability across questions. Param: limit. Transmission: drives / driven_by (other factors, sign +1 = same direction, with a rationale) and industries (GICS industries whose earnings it moves — directly for a niche factor, via a niche factor for a macro one; sign = effect on earnings if the factor rises).

    How it is measured (factor_betas.py): the factor's two-day move after each day a question's odds jumped ≥ 5 points and held, regressed on the jump, then shrunk toward questions sharing the factor and channel (empirical Bayes). A question with no jumps gets the pooled estimate. Jump days also carry other news, and ICE credit spreads on FRED start in late 2023.

    GET/factors/{id}/history

    Daily factor levels (default: the last year). Params: start, end. Spreads and yields are in percent.

    Factor research

    What the Factor research tab uses. Every link and number carries evidence: definition (reference data) · observed (captured prices / levels) · qualitative (rule, transmission or analyst-assessed link, not measured) · estimated (regression; in-sample association, not causal) · validated (none exist yet).

    GET/factors/{id}/securities

    Securities with a measured loading on the factor (latest security_betas.py run), |t| first: beta (weekly % move per unit weekly factor move, controlling for the market), se, ci_low / ci_high (95%), t, p, q (Benjamini–Hochberg across the run), n_weeks; spec and run. Params: min_abs_t, limit. A factor without a price series returns none, with a note.

    GET/securities/{ticker}/regression

    A joint regression of the security's weekly returns on the market plus the chosen factors (factor=rates_us_10y&factor=brent), with the same estimator as the stored loadings, computed now and not stored: coefficients with se, 95% CI, t, p, the stored single-factor beta for comparison, R², adjusted R², residual SD, weeks and window; missing lists factors without enough data.

    GET/markets/{id}/history

    Captured snapshots of one contract (default: last 30 days): ts, price and price_rule (mid if the spread ≤ 10¢, else last trade), bid, ask, last, volume, open interest, tier (backfill = rebuilt from venue history later). Params: start, end, limit. /factors/{id}/history points now also carry fetched_at, n_vintages and source; /securities/{ticker} loadings carry the CI, p and q.

    GICS industries

    GET/industries

    The 74 GICS industries (id gics:453010, code, name, sector, industry_group) with n_issuers and n_factors (niche drivers). Param: sector.

    GET/industries/{code}

    One industry (453010, gics:453010 or a sub-industry code): sub_industries, issuers, drivers (niche factors with side, sign, the sub_industries they apply to and the factors driven_by) and the questions that reach it through those factors (live first; via = the connecting factors). /questions/{id} has the reverse view: industries with the paths question → factor [→ niche factor] → industry.

    GICS sectors (the GICS Dashboard tab)

    GET/gics

    The 11 GICS sectors (code, name, etf = its SPDR sector ETF, n_linked, n_measured) with the top limit (default 10) live events that move each. Which events concern a sector is an LLM judgment (llm: sign = what YES means for the sector, strength 1–3, rationale), a placeholder for a methodical mapping. How much each moves it is measured: sector_move = the ETF's % move per +1.0 of probability (per_10pts, move_if_yes / move_if_no, implied, direct, n_events, agrees_with_llm); events are ranked by it, unmeasured ones last. Each event has prob, change_24h, change_1w, change_1m (headline rung, points as fractions) and lead_factor: its most relevant factor for the sector (why), with that factor's measured sensitivity on the event (as on /questions/{id}) and the ETF's etf_loading on it.

    GET/gics/{sector}

    One sector by code (45) or name (Energy), up to limit (default 50) events.

    Issuer and Macro dashboards

    Dense measurements (event_betas.py, daily): every issuer's primary security and every macro factor with a daily series, regressed against every event with data — jump days on any of its contracts, or (securities) measured factor moves — not only linked pairs. The top 50 live events per target are stored.

    GET/dashboard/issuers

    Every issuer (S&P 500 plus the other borrowers): id, name, sector, ticker, n_evaluated (events its stock was measured against), n_live.

    GET/dashboard/issuers/{id}

    One issuer (id, QID or exact name): events = the top limit (default 20) live events with an economic channels to it (factors · entity · sector · analyst), ranked by |move|; statistical = the largest associations with no known channel (possibly spurious). Each has prob, change_24h / change_1w / change_1m, move (% per +1.0: per_10pts, move_if_yes / move_if_no, implied, direct, n_events, q = Benjamini–Hochberg q-value, events) and lead_factor. coverage counts what was measured.

    GET/dashboard/factors

    Every macro factor: series (null = monthly / quarterly indicator, can't be regressed on daily jumps), n_evaluated, n_live.

    GET/dashboard/factors/{id}

    One macro factor and its top limit live events by |move|: sensitivity in the factor's units per +1.0 (per_10pts, own_beta, own_se, q, linked, channel, events). Linked pairs are shrunk toward their pooled channel estimate (as factor_betas), unlinked ones toward 0. Factors without a series list their linked events unmeasured.

    Calibration

    GET/calibration

    How often a price came true: binary markets that resolved yes/no, priced horizon days before resolution (1, 7, 30 or 90; default 7). Per probability bin: mean price vs share resolved YES; brier score and brier_skill (vs always predicting the base rate); the same by_venue and by_liquidity (open interest or 24h volume then). Params: horizon, venue, scope = graph (markets in the graph, default) · all, bins. The sample covers markets the collector saw resolve.

Params: sort = score (default) · impact · risk, min_score (default 0.1), limit, include_closed.

    Securities (measured)

    How much a security moves with a factor or a question is measured from prices (security_betas.py), not written down: weekly factor loadings controlling for the market, and per question a factor-implied move (the question's measured factor moves × the security's joint loadings) and a direct market-adjusted two-day move on the question's jump days, shrunk toward the implied one. A pair is measured when the security loads significantly (|t| ≥ 3) on one of the question's factors, or when an entity path reaches its issuer (found_by = factor · structural · both).

    GET/securities

    All securities (ticker, name, asset class, country, issuer — none for funds) with how many questions have a measured sensitivity. Param: q.

    GET/securities/{ticker}

    GET/securities/{ticker}/exposures

Params: sort = risk · move · evidence, min_events, limit, include_closed. /questions/{id} lists the 15 securities that move most with it.
