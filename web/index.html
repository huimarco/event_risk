    What this is

    A map from geopolitical questions people bet on to the investments those questions affect. Prediction markets put a live probability on events like "Will China blockade Taiwan in 2026?". This graph connects each question to the countries, people, companies and topics it involves, and from there to stocks, ETFs and currencies — so a risk desk can see which geopolitical events its portfolio is exposed to, and how their odds are moving.

    How every connection is made

    Everything is produced by algorithms from public data — no AI models, no hand-picked lists — except one input, marked below.

    ConnectionExampleHow it is made
    Market → PropositionKalshi and Polymarket "Trump visits Taiwan in 2026" are one question Contract terms compared field by field, then confirmed by prices (below)
    Proposition ↔ Proposition"Invasion by June 2027" implies "by Dec 2027" Logic from the contracts: implies nested deadlines or thresholds, mutex can't both happen (venue flags), near_equivalent same event, materially different terms
    Proposition → Entity"…China invade Taiwan…" involves China, Taiwan, invasion Phrases in the question linked to Wikipedia articles
    Entity ↔ EntityXi Jinping — citizen of — China Facts from Wikidata, strength from Wikipedia co-occurrence (below)
    Entity → SecurityTaiwan → TSM Hand-written analyst estimates — the only manual input, to be replaced by company revenue data and measured price reactions

    Markets → propositions: comparing contracts, not wording

    Questions that read alike are often different bets ("visits China before May 14" vs "on May 14"), so text similarity is not used. Each market is parsed into a contract signature from the venue's data and its templated rules text:

    FieldExample: "Will Trump visit Taiwan before Jan 1, 2027?"
    Actionvisit — from a fixed vocabulary of event types
    Actor → targetDonald Trump → Taiwan (which side of the verb matters)
    Time windowDec 2, 2025 – Dec 31, 2026, read exactly from the rules
    Threshold— (used for "tariff ≥ 20%"-style questions)
    Conditionsphysically present (others: in person, incl. phone calls, photographed, becomes law…)

    Two markets are the same question only if every field matches. Then comes a final check with real money: if their prices differ by more than 10 points, the market is saying they are different bets, so they are linked but not merged. Each pair is judged once, using prices at the moment the newer market was listed, so the decision never flips later. Example: "Trump, Xi and Putin meet" (Kalshi, 34.5%) vs "are seen together" (Polymarket, 84.5%) — linked as near-equivalent, never blended.

    Entities: discovered from the markets, connected through Wikipedia

    There is no fixed entity list; it is regenerated from the markets on every update.

    • Discovery. Phrases in each question are looked up as Wikipedia titles ("People's Republic of China" → China, "household registration system" → Hukou).
    • Type and parent. Wikidata says what each entity is (person, country, organisation, concept) and which country it belongs to (Trump → United States), so "Trump makes a deal" and "the U.S. makes a deal" are recognised as the same side.
    • Concepts are kept only if specific and relevant — mentioned in under 1% of Wikipedia articles and related to the question's actors ("tariff" yes, "company" no).
    • Strength of connection — how often Wikipedia articles mention both, relative to how often each is mentioned (the Normalized Google Distance formula, computed on Wikipedia):
    relatedness(A, B) = 1 − [ log max(|A|,|B|) − log |A∩B| ] / [ log W − log min(|A|,|B|) ]
    |A|, |B| = articles mentioning each · |A∩B| = articles mentioning both · W = all articles (~7.3M)

    Example: Putin–Xi Jinping 0.66, China–Taiwan 0.56. Links need at least 10 articles mentioning both, and a score of 0.25 or more.

    Numbers you will see

    ProbabilityThe latest market price of each venue's contract, averaged (weighted by open interest when available). Only same-question markets are averaged.
    24h changeProbability now minus 24 hours earlier.
    Exposure scoreHow strongly a security is connected to a question through the graph (product of link strengths along the path). It ranks relevance, not impact: it does not say whether an event helps or hurts, or by how much.

    Time travel

    As of shows the graph and probabilities as they were at any past moment. Nothing is overwritten: every change is stored with when it became true and when the system learned it, so a past view uses only what was known then — safe for backtesting.

    Data and updates

    MarketsKalshi and Polymarket public APIs — a snapshot every 15 minutes (in practice every few hours on the free scheduler), plus hourly history back to 2021
    GraphUpdated only when new markets are listed (or a contract's terms change); price updates never alter it. Each new market adds its own connections; existing ones stay as they were
    EntitiesWikipedia and Wikidata, cached after the first lookup
    Security pricesDaily closes from Yahoo Finance (research use only)

    Current limits

    • Entity → security weights are analyst estimates, not sourced figures.
    • Exposure scores have no direction or size yet; measured "event betas" (how prices actually moved when odds jumped) are the next step.
    • Scope is one theme for now: US–China, Taiwan and semiconductors.
