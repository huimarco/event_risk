    What this is

    A map from geopolitical questions people bet on to the investments those questions affect. Prediction markets put a live probability on events like "Will China blockade Taiwan in 2026?". This graph connects each question to the countries, people, companies and topics it involves, and from there to stocks, ETFs and currencies — so a risk desk can see which geopolitical events its portfolio is exposed to, and how their odds are moving.

    Scope: every geopolitical market. Polymarket's geopolitics section is taken as the venue classifies it. A Kalshi world or politics market counts as geopolitical when it involves a foreign state actor — a country other than the US, or a person or organisation belonging to one (via Wikidata): "Will Trump visit China?" is in, "Will the Senate confirm…?" is out.

    How every connection is made

    Everything is produced by algorithms from public data — no AI models, no hand-picked lists — except the analyst estimates marked below.

    ConnectionExampleHow it is made
    Market → PropositionKalshi and Polymarket "Trump visits Taiwan in 2026" are one question Contract terms compared field by field, then confirmed by prices (below)
    Proposition ↔ Proposition"Invasion by June 2027" implies "by Dec 2027" Logic from the contracts: implies nested deadlines or thresholds, mutex can't both happen (venue flags), near_equivalent same event, materially different terms
    Proposition → Entity"…China invade Taiwan…" involves China (actor), Taiwan (target), invasion Phrases in the question linked to Wikipedia articles; each link carries the entity's role and what a YES means for it (below)
    Entity ↔ EntityXi Jinping — citizen of — China Facts from Wikidata, strength from Wikipedia co-occurrence (below)
    Entity → SecurityTaiwan → TSM Hand-written analyst estimates — the only manual input, to be replaced by company revenue data and measured price reactions — plus which industries gain from escalation (defense, oil producers)

    Markets → propositions: comparing contracts, not wording

    Questions that read alike are often different bets ("visits China before May 14" vs "on May 14"), so text similarity is not used. Each market is parsed into a contract signature from the venue's data and its templated rules text:

    FieldExample: "Will Trump visit Taiwan before Jan 1, 2027?"
    Actionvisit — from a fixed vocabulary of event types
    Actor → targetDonald Trump → Taiwan (which side of the verb matters)
    Time windowDec 2, 2025 – Dec 31, 2026, read exactly from the rules
    Threshold— (used for "tariff ≥ 20%"-style questions)
    Conditionsphysically present (others: in person, incl. phone calls, photographed, becomes law…)

    On the same venue, the resolution rules are compared too: two markets with identical questions whose rules name different things (e.g. different intersections in "Will Russia capture Novopavlivka?") are different bets. A negated question ("…agrees not to join NATO", "military action ends") is never matched with the plain one.

    Two markets are the same question only if every field matches. Then comes a final check with real money: if their prices differ by more than 10 points, the market is saying they are different bets, so they are linked but not merged. Each pair is judged once, using prices at the moment the newer market was listed, so the decision never flips later. Example: "Trump, Xi and Putin meet" (Kalshi, 34.5%) vs "are seen together" (Polymarket, 84.5%) — linked as near-equivalent, never blended.

    Entities: discovered from the markets, connected through Wikipedia

    There is no fixed entity list; it is regenerated from the markets on every update.

    • Discovery. Phrases in each question are looked up as Wikipedia titles ("People's Republic of China" → China, "household registration system" → Hukou).
    • Type and parent. Wikidata says what each entity is (person, country, organisation, concept) and which country it belongs to (Trump → United States), so "Trump makes a deal" and "the U.S. makes a deal" are recognised as the same side.
    • Concepts are kept only if specific and relevant — mentioned in under 1% of Wikipedia articles and related to the question's actors ("tariff" yes, "company" no).
    • Strength of connection — how often Wikipedia articles mention both, relative to how often each is mentioned (the Normalized Google Distance formula, computed on Wikipedia):
    relatedness(A, B) = 1 − [ log max(|A|,|B|) − log |A∩B| ] / [ log W − log min(|A|,|B|) ]
    |A|, |B| = articles mentioning each · |A∩B| = articles mentioning both · W = all articles (~7.3M)

    Example: Putin–Xi Jinping 0.66, China–Taiwan 0.56. Links need at least 10 articles mentioning both, and a score of 0.25 or more.

    Every market's outcome — merged, linked, unmatched or unparsed, with its parsed signature and the reason each near miss was rejected — is listed in the explorer's Markets tab.

    Direction and size of impact

    Each proposition → entity link records what a YES means for that entity: the action (from the contract signature) sets a channel (conflict, trade, sanctions, instability, diplomacy), a severity, and a direction for the target and for the actor — an invasion is adverse for the target (−1.0) and somewhat adverse for the invader (−0.6); a ceasefire or trade deal is favourable for both. Negated questions flip it. Each entity → security link says how the security responds on each channel: by default it suffers with the entity, but defense contractors gain from conflict and oil producers gain from supply disruption.

    path strength = severity × role weight × pass-through × exposure weight · sign = direction for the entity × response on that channel
    impact(security, proposition) = Σ over paths of sign × strength · relevance = 1 − Π(1 − strength) · risk = |impact| × √(p(1−p))
    pass-through = 1 for the entity itself, 0.5 × link strength for a linked entity (one hop)

    Impact is in graph units, not returns: backtest.py measures how it maps to actual abnormal returns when odds jump, and whether its direction is right. Risk is largest for events near 50% — a 99% event can't move much more.

    Numbers you will see

    ProbabilityThe latest market price of each venue's contract, averaged (weighted by open interest when available). Only same-question markets are averaged.
    24h changeProbability now minus 24 hours earlier.
    RelevanceHow strongly a security is connected to a question through the graph, 0–1, combining every path.
    ImpactSigned: ▲ the security is expected to rise if the event happens, ▼ to fall. A structural prior, not a measured beta.
    Risk|impact| × √(p(1−p)): how much the question can still move the security given today's odds.

    Time travel

    As of shows the graph and probabilities as they were at any past moment. Nothing is overwritten: every change is stored with when it became true and when the system learned it, so a past view uses only what was known then — safe for backtesting.

    Data and updates

    MarketsKalshi and Polymarket public APIs — a snapshot every 15 minutes (in practice every few hours on the free scheduler), plus hourly history back to 2021
    GraphUpdated only when new markets are listed (or a contract's terms change); price updates never alter it. Each new market adds its own connections; existing ones stay as they were
    EntitiesWikipedia and Wikidata, cached after the first lookup
    Security pricesDaily closes from Yahoo Finance (research use only)

    Current limits

    • Entity → security weights are analyst estimates, not sourced figures.
    • Direction and impact come from a fixed table of actions and industry responses; about one market in eight uses an action outside the vocabulary and get relevance without direction. Measured "event betas" (how prices actually moved when odds jumped) are the next step.
    • Securities and their exposure weights still cover one theme (US–China, Taiwan, semiconductors), so many propositions have no linked securities yet.

    Using the API

    Everything in the explorer is available as JSON over HTTP. Base URL: BASE. Interactive reference with a "try it" button for every endpoint: /docs.

    Basics

    AuthenticationIf the server has API keys configured, send yours in the X-API-Key header. A missing or wrong key returns 401.
    Time travelEvery read accepts as_of (the world at that moment) and known_as_of (only what the graph had recorded by then), as ISO 8601 timestamps, e.g. 2026-06-01T00:00:00Z. Both default to now. Set both to the same time for a point-in-time, backtest-safe view.
    IDsPropositions prop:polymarket:665270 · entities wd:Q865 (Wikidata id; Taiwan) · securities sec:TSM · markets mkt:kalshi:KXTAIWANLVL4-29JAN01. Get them from the list endpoints.
    Probabilities0–1. prob is the blended price of a proposition's markets; change_24h is in probability units (0.05 = 5 points).
    Live onlyList endpoints return propositions priced in the last 7 days unless include_closed=true.
    Errors401 bad key · 404 id not found at that as_of / known_as_of · 422 invalid parameter.

    Quick start

    curl -H "X-API-Key: $KEY" "BASE/propositions?q=taiwan&sort=move&limit=5"
    import requests
api = requests.Session(); api.headers["X-API-Key"] = KEY      # omit if the server has no keys
top = api.get("BASE/propositions", params={"sort": "move", "limit": 10}).json()
for p in top["propositions"]:
    print(f'{p["prob"]:.1%}  {p["change_24h"] or 0:+.1%}  {p["name"]}')

    Propositions

    GET/propositions

    List propositions with probability and 24h change. Params: q (name search), entity (only those involving this entity id), sort = move (largest |24h change|, default) · prob · name, limit (≤ 500), include_closed.

    { "count": 151, "propositions": [
    { "id": "prop:polymarket:567621", "name": "Will China invade Taiwan by end of 2026?",
      "prob": 0.0215, "prob_24h_ago": 0.0205, "change_24h": 0.001,
      "close_time": "2027-01-01 04:59:00+00:00", "venues": ["polymarket"] } ] }
    GET/propositions/{id}

    One proposition: its venue markets (price, rules text), the entities it involves, and logical links (implies, mutex, near_equivalent) to other propositions, with the reason for each.

    GET/propositions/{id}/history

    Blended probability over time. Params: start, end (default: last 30 days), interval = 1h · 1d. Each point: {"t", "prob", "n_markets"}.

    Graph

    GET/graph/{node_id}

    The neighbourhood of any proposition, entity or security, for visualisation: nodes (id, type, name, depth, prob) and edges (src, dst, type, weight, provenance, note). Params: depth (1–3), fanout (strongest links kept per node).

    GET/entities

    All entities, optionally type = person · country · organisation_or_place · concept, with how many propositions involve each.

    GET/entities/{id}

    One entity: Wikidata description and Wikipedia link, propositions involving it, linked entities (with relation and strength), and securities exposed to it.

    Securities and portfolios

    GET/securities

    All securities (ticker, name, asset class, country, sector, index) and how many exposure links each has.

    GET/securities/{ticker}/exposures

    Propositions a security is exposed to, summed over every path through the graph: score (relevance 0–1), impact (signed; > 0 = expected to rise if the event happens; null when no path has a known direction), direction (up · down · unknown), risk (|impact| × √(p(1−p))) and the three strongest paths with their channel and sign. Params: sort = score (default) · impact · risk, min_score (default 0.1), limit, include_closed.

    { "security": { "id": "sec:TSM", "name": "Taiwan Semiconductor (ADR)" },
  "exposures": [ { "id": "prop:polymarket:567621", "name": "Will China invade Taiwan by December 31, 2027?",
                   "prob": 0.095, "score": 0.95, "impact": -1.46, "direction": "down", "risk": 0.43, "n_paths": 4,
                   "path": [ {"id": "prop:polymarket:567621"}, {"id": "wd:Q865", "name": "Taiwan"}, {"id": "sec:TSM"} ],
                   "paths": [ { "strength": 0.9, "sign": -1.0, "channel": "conflict", "role": "target", "nodes": [ … ] } ] } ] }
    POST/portfolio/scan

    Send holdings, get back the geopolitical propositions the portfolio is most exposed to: exposure = Σ weight × relevance score; impact = Σ weight × signed impact (holdings on opposite sides offset), with direction and risk. moved flags propositions whose probability moved at least move_threshold in 24h.

    curl -X POST "BASE/portfolio/scan" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -H "X-API-Key: $KEY" \
  -d '{"holdings": [{"ticker": "TSM", "weight": 0.10}, {"ticker": "NVDA", "weight": 0.08}],
       "move_threshold": 0.05, "limit": 10}'
    { "unknown_tickers": [], "count": 150, "propositions": [
    { "id": "prop:polymarket:567621", "name": "Will China invade Taiwan by end of 2026?", "prob": 0.0215,
      "change_24h": 0.001, "exposure": 0.177, "moved": false,
      "holdings": [ { "ticker": "TSM", "weight": 0.1, "score": 0.9, "via": ["Taiwan"] } ] } ] }

    Markets (match review)

    GET/markets

    Venue markets by how they fared in cross-venue matching: merged · linked · unmatched · unparsed. Params: status (repeatable; default unmatched + unparsed), q, venue, limit, include_closed. counts covers every status.

    GET/markets/{id}

    One market: status, parsed contract signature, parser issues, nearest rejected candidates with the reason for each, rules text and its proposition.

    Other

    GET/health

    Service status and when data was last collected successfully.
